Business Question 1
Which region has the worst on-time delivery performance, and what's actually driving it?

Executive Answer
Based on the analysis-ready shipment dataset (3,444 completed, valid deliveries), Central recorded the lowest observed on-time delivery rate at 48.33%, while South recorded the highest at 52.42%.
However, the difference between the best- and worst-performing regions is only 4.09 percentage points, and statistical testing found no significant association between region and delivery performance (Chi-square test, p = 0.757).
Therefore, there is insufficient evidence to conclude that geography itself is driving delivery performance. The observed regional differences are small and are consistent with normal operational variation.

Conclusion
The analysis indicates that regional geography is not a major driver of delivery performance.
Although West recorded the highest on-time rate (51.3%) and Central the lowest (48.3%), the difference between regions was less than 3 percentage points after excluding the underrepresented South region. Confidence intervals overlapped substantially across all major regions, indicating that these differences are unlikely to represent meaningful operational variation. This conclusion was further supported by the Chi-square test (p = 0.757), which found no statistically significant association between region and delivery outcomes.
When performance was examined within distance bands, the four primary regions continued to exhibit similar on-time rates, suggesting that shipment distance does not materially alter regional performance. The apparent variability observed in South was attributed to its small sample size (124 shipments), resulting in high statistical uncertainty rather than a true operational effect.
A more important finding emerged when carrier performance was analysed within each region. Differences between carriers frequently exceeded 15–20 percentage points in on-time performance, substantially larger than the differences observed between regions themselves. Several carriers consistently outperformed the national average regardless of region, while others consistently underperformed.
Overall, the evidence suggests that carrier selection—not geographic region—is the dominant operational factor influencing delivery performance. Regional performance is broadly consistent across the network, whereas carrier-level differences provide far greater explanatory power and represent a more actionable opportunity for operational improvement.
 Business Recommendations
Rather than implementing region-specific interventions, management should prioritize network-wide operational improvements.
Recommended actions include:
Investigate carrier-level performance to identify consistently underperforming carriers regardless of region.
Monitor customer-level delay patterns to determine whether delays are concentrated among specific accounts or shipping profiles.
Track network-wide on-time rate weekly, since performance appears to vary more over time than across geographic regions.
Use regional KPIs for monitoring rather than optimization, as current evidence does not justify region-specific operational changes.
